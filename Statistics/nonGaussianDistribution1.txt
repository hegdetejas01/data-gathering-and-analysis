### UNIFORM DISTRIBUTION ###    
    * It is a probability distribution where all outcomes are "equally" likely within a given range
    * Spinning a die once - probability of getting 1,2,3,4,5,6 are equal and is 1/6.
    * Skewess = 0

    * 2 Types
        1. Discrete Uniform Distribution: spinning a die once
        2. Continuous Uniform Distribution

    * Notation
        X ~ u(a,b)
            - a is the lower value in the range = 1 for rolling a die
            - b is the higher value in the range = 6 for rolling a die

    Egs of continuous uniform distribution
        1. The height of a person randomly selected from a group of individuals whose heights range from 5.6 feet to 6.0 feet would follow a continuous uniform distribution.

        2. The time it takes for a machine to produce a product, where the production time ranges from 5 to 10 minutes, would follow a continuous uniform distribution.

        * probability density = 1/(b-a)
        * f(x) = 
                    1/(b-a) for a <= x <= b 
                    0 elsewhere

### Log Normal Distribution ###
    * It is a heavy tailed(right skewed) continuous probability distribution of a random variable whose logarithm is normally distributed
    * Log of this distribution is Normally Distributed
    * The dataset which is right skewed and their log is a normal distribution

    Eg: 
        1. Length of comments posted in Internet discussion forums follows a log-normal distribution
        2. Users' dwell time on online articles (jokes, news etc.) follows a log-normal distribution
        3. The length of chess games tends to follow a log-normal distribution
        4. In economics, there is evidence that the income of 97%–99% of the population is distributed log-normally

    Notation:

        X ~ lognormal(mu, std), provided that ln(X) ~ N(mu` , std`)

    Formula: 

        f(X) = ((e ^ (-1/2)*((ln(x)-mu)/std)**2)/(std * X * sqrt(2*pi)))

    CDF of lognormal:
        * graph : nonGaussianDistribution1.pdf

    Checking Lognormal Distribution:
        I have a dataset X, 
        Calculate Log of X,
        QQPlot for log(X) and Y, where Y is normally distributed,
        If Y and log(X) are on the same line, then log(X) is normally distributed,
        Thus, X is lognormal distributed


### Pareto Distribution ###
    * It is commonly used to model the distribution of wealth, income, and other quantities that exhibit a similar power-law behaviour, i.e. it is a special case of power-law

    Power-Law : 
        - It is a functional relationship between two variables, where one variable is proportional to a power of the other.

        - If x and y are power variable then,   y = k * x^a

        - 80:20 Rule = Whenever something is following power law, 20 percent will have 80 percent occupancy of the result
            -- larger portion of the wealth of any society is owned by a smaller percentage of the people in that society
            -- "80-20 rule" which says that 20% of the population controls 80% of the wealth

    Notation:
        X ~ Pr(alpha)

    PDeF:
        * Graph: nonGaussianDistribution1.pdf
        * Greater the alpha, greater will be the height of the graph in Y axis
        * Greater the alpha, less will be the thickness of the tail (thin tail)
        * When alpha = infinity, pareto curve is a line parallel to Y axis
        * skewed

        Eg:
            The sizes of human settlements
            File size distribution of Internet traffic which uses the TCP protocol

    NOTE: Only when alpha is 1.16, then 80-20 rule is applicable

    Formula:
        pdf = (alpha*(Xm^alpha))/(x^(alpha+1))

    CDF:
        * Graph: nonGaussianDistribution1.pdf

    Checking Pareto Distribution:
        * Log-Log Plot
        * y = (alpha*(Xm^alpha))/(x^(alpha+1))
        * take log of x and log of y 
        * plot graph, if it has a negative slope and is a straight line (nonGaussianDistribution1.py), then it is pareto distribution




###### Transformation ######
    * Converting the non-gaussian distribution which is normal like to exactly normal distribution
    * Log Transformation and Box-Cox Transformation
    * 3 types of transformer
        1. Fucntion transformer : log, reciprocal, square/sqrt, custom
        2. Power transformer : Box-Cox, Yeo-Johnson
        3. Quantile transformer

    ### Log Transform ###
        * log(x)
        * Log of every value in the dataset, it will become more normally distributed
        * It doesn't work on negative values
        * Works on Right Skewed Data

    ### Reciprocal Transform ###
        * 1/x

    ### Square Transform ###
        * x**2
        * used for left skewed data
    
    ### Square Root Transform ###
        * sqrt(x)

    ### Box-Cox Transform ###
        * Converting to normal distribution

        xi^(lambda) =   (xi^(lambda)-1)/(lambda), if lambda != 0
                        (ln(xi)), if lambda = 0

        * Lambda varies from -5 to 5. In this process, we try all differenct possibilities of lambda and we choose the optimal value (resulting in the best approximation to tha normal distribution) for your variable.

        * It is done by - maximum likelihood or bayesian

        * xi should be greater than 0

    ### Yeo-Johnson Transform ###
        * It is the modification of box-cox to make it applicable for negative and 0 values as well
