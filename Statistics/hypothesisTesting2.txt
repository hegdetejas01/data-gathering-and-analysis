# P-Value
    * It is the probablility of getting a sample as or more extreme(having more evidence against H0) than our own sample, given that the null hypothesis is true.
    
    Eg:
        Tossing a coin for 100 times
        H0: p(H) = p(T) # probability of getting heads and tails are same
        Ha: p(H) > p(T)

        Suppose, if i get 53 heads and 47 tails in my first 100 trails, we can't conclude, Ha is true

        P-Value here is: probability of heading coming greater than 53 - because it is our own sample. It is the area of the graph at and beyond 53. P-Value for 53 heads is 0.3, that means - 

            If  the null hypothesis is true, and if i am conducting an experiment of tossing a coin 100 times for 100 times and if the p-value is 0.3 it means, 30 out of 100 times (of tossing a coin 100 times), i may get 53 heads 

    * Rejecting the Null Hypothesis:
        if p-value <= alpha: Reject null hypothesis
        
        if alpha is not known   
            - if p<0.01: can reject the null hypothesis
            - if 0.01<=p<0.05: can reject the null hypothesis
            - if 0.05<=p<=0.1: more investigation needed
            - if p>=0.1: no evidence against null hypothesis, null hypothesis can't be rejeceted

    
# P-Value in the context of Z-Test
    (Instead of rejection region approach, we use p-value approach)
    
    Examples: 
        1. Suppose a company is evaluating the impact of a new training program on the productivity of its employees. The company has data on the average productivity of its employees before implementing the training program. The average productivity was 50 units per day. After implementing the training program, the company measures the productivity of a random sample of 30 employees. The sample has an average productivity of 53 units per day and the pop std is 4. The company wants to know if the new training program has significantly increased productivity.

            # Using Rejection Region Approcah

                Population mean (mu) = 50
                sample size (n) = 30
                sample mean (xbar) = 53
                population std = 4
                alpha = 0.05

                H0: mu = 50
                Ha: mu > 50

                Since n>30, we can apply CLT and the distribution is a normal.

                Z   = (xbar - mu) / (std/sqrt(n))
                    = (53-50) * (4/sqrt(30))
                    = 4.10

                z = 1.65

                ** Z falls beyond 1.65 and hence I reject the null hypothesis

                - Even if we have Z=1.66 we reject and even if Z=15 we reject: This doesn't tell the strenght of the evidence

            
            # Using P-Value Approcah

                Z=4.10 (from above)
                Check the area of the graph right to the point of Z=4.10
                Check Z-Table, area before 4.10 is 0.999 and hence area beyond Z=4.10 is 0.001 

                p-value = 0.001
                p-value < alpha : Reject the Null Hypothesis

                If Z=1.7, p-value = 0.05

                Therefore, i am more sure when Z=4.1 than Z=1.7

        2. Suppose a snack food company claims that their Lays wafer packets contain an average weight of 50 grams per packet. To verify this claim, a consumer watchdog organization decides to test a random sample of Lays wafer packets. The organization wants to determine whether the actual average weight differs significantly from the claimed 50 grams. The organization collects a random sample of 40 Lays wafer packets and measures their weights. They find that the sample has an average weight of 49 grams, with a pop standard eviation of 5 grams.

            mu = 50
            n = 40
            xbar = 49
            std = 5
            alpha = 0.05

            H0: mu = 50
            H1: mu != 50 # two tailed

            Z   = (xbar - mu) / (std/sqrt(n))
                = -1 * (5/sqrt(40))
                = -1.26

            Consider, -1.26 and +1.26 on the graph 
            Consider area1 = area of the graph behind the point -1.26 and area2 = area of the graph beyond the point +1.26
            
            p-value = area1 + area2 = 2 * area1
                    = 2 * 0.103
                    = 0.206

            since, p-value > 0.05, I can't reject the null hypothesis



### t-Test ###

    * It uses the sample std deviation and works well even for smaller sample size (z-test is used when sigma -- pop std is known)
    * It is used in hypothesis testing to compare the means of two samples or to compare a sample mean to a known population mean
    * It is based on the t-distribution
    
    Types of T-Test:
        1. One Sample t-test
            - Used on one sample
            - Mean of the sample, is used to do hypothesis testing on the known population mean
            - The null hypothesis states that there is no significant difference between the sample mean and the population mean, while the alternative hypothesis tates that there is a significant difference
        
        2. Independent Two Sample t-test
            - Used to compare the means of two independent samples
            - The null hypothesis states that there is no significant difference between the means of the two samples, while the alternative hypothesis states that there is a significant difference

        3. Paired t-test (dependent two sample t-test)
            - Used to compare the means of two samples that are dependent or paired such as pre-test or post-test scores of the same group of subjects/measurement taken under two different conditions.
            - The null hypothesis states that there is no significant difference between the means of the paired differences, while the alternative hypothesis states that there is a significant difference.


# One sample t-test
    * A one-sample t-test checks whether a sample mean differs from the population mean

    Assumptions:
        1. Normality - either population or sample
        2. Independent obersavtions
        3. Random sampling
        4. Unknown population std

    Example:
    Suppose a manufacturer claims that the average weight of their new chocolate bars is 50 grams, we highly doubt that and want to check this so we drew out a sample of 25 chocolate bars and measured their weight, the sample mean came out to be 49.7 grams and the sample std deviation was 1.2 grams. Consider the significance level to be 0.05
        mu = 50
        n = 25
        xbar = 49.7
        alpha = 0.05
        sample std (s) = 1.2

        degree of freedom = n-1 = 24

        H0: mu = 50
        Ha: mu != 50

        Assuming that the data is normal

        t = (xbar - mu) / (s/sqrt(n))
          = (49.7 - 50) / (1.2/sqrt(25))
          = -1.5/1.2
          = -1.25

        Consider, -1.25 and +1.25 on the graph 
        Consider area1 = area of the graph behind the point -1.25 and area2 = area of the graph beyond the point +1.25
        
        Use CDF to find the area left to -1.25, n-1=24

        p-value = area1 + area2 = 2 * area1
                = 2 * 0.11
                = 0.22

        p-value > alpha: Null hypothesis can't be rejeceted

# Independent Two Sample t-test
    * It is used to compare the means of two independent groups to determine if there is a significant difference between them

    Assumption:
        1. Independent Observations
        2. Population Std unavailable
        3. Normality
        4. Random Sampling
        5. Equal variance (homoscedasticity) - 
            Variance of one Sample = Variance of the Second Sample 
            Levine test

    Example:
        Suppose a website owner claims that there is no difference in the average time spent on their website between desktop and mobile users. To test this claim, we collect data from 30 desktop users and 30 mobile users regarding the time spent on the website in minutes. The sample statistics are as follows:
            
            desktop users = [12, 15, 18, 16, 20, 17, 14, 22, 19, 21, 23, 18, 25, 17, 16, 24, 20, 19, 22, 18, 15, 14, 23, 16, 12, 21, 19, 17, 20, 14]
            
            mobile_users = [10, 12, 14, 13, 16, 15, 11, 17, 14, 16, 18, 14, 20, 15, 14, 19, 16, 15, 17, 14, 12, 11, 18, 15, 10, 16, 15, 13, 16, 11]

        Mobile Users:
            Sample size (n2): 30
            Sample mean (mean2): 14.3 minutes
            Sample standard deviation (std_dev2): 2.7 minutes

        Desktop Users:
            Sample size (n1): 30
            Sample mean (mean1): 18.5 minutes
            Sample standard deviation (std_dev1): 3.5 minutes

        H0: mu(mobile) = mu(desktop)
        Ha: mu(mobile) != mu(desktop)

        t = (x1bar - x2bar) / sqrt((s1^2/n1) + (s2^2/n2))
          = (18.5 - 14.3) / sqrt((3.5^2/30) + (2.7^7/30))
          = 4.2 / (sqrt(19.54/30))
          = 5.25

        Two tailed test = plot the graph and identify -5.25 and +5.25 from the t-Table


# Paired Two Sample t-Test
    * It is used to compare the means of the two related or dependent groups

    Common scenarios where a paired two-sample t-test is used include:
        1. Before-and-after studies
        2. Matched or correlated groups

    Assumptions:
        1. Paired obersavtions - Two set should be related to each other.
        2. Normality - The difference between the paired observations should be normally distributed.
        3. Independence of pairs - Each pair should be independent of each other

    Example:
        Let's assume that a fitness center is evaluating the effectiveness of a new 8 -week weight loss program. They enroll 15 participants in the program and measure their weights before and after the program. The goal is to test whether the new weight loss program leads to a significant reduction in the participants' weight

        Before the program: [80, 92, 75, 68, 85, 78, 73, 90, 70, 88, 76, 84, 82, 77, 91]
        After the program: [78, 93, 81, 67, 88, 76, 74, 91, 69, 88, 77, 81, 80, 79, 88]

        Significance level = 0.05

            H0: mu(before) = mu(after)
            Ha: mu(before) > mu(after)
            
            Differences in weight should be normal
            Then find sample mean and sample std of the differences

            mudiff = mubefore - muafter = 0
            t = xbardiff - mudiff / ((std of diff)/sqrt(n))
              = xbardiff / (std diff/sqrt(15))
